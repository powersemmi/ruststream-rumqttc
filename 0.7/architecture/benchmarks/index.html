    Benchmarks

    What ruststream-rumqttc costs on every message: throughput against the raw rumqttc client, and instructions and allocations of its code.

    Against a raw client​

    The first chart runs each scenario three ways on a real MQTT server. Raw client drives rumqttc directly. Broker crate drives this crate's subscription with a loop of its own and no runtime. RustStream is the service a user writes, a #[subscriber] handler in an app. The first gap is what this crate costs over the client it wraps, and the second is what the runtime adds on top.

    Each loop is a bar from its best round to its slowest, with a dot at the best round and a notch at the median. The zero of the axis is the raw client's best round, and a dot right of zero is slower. A hollow dot is a loop whose difference from the raw client is smaller than the spread of the rounds. The table under the chart prints the best round with the median in parentheses.

    The two scenarios are the two qualities of service a service reads at. At QoS 0 the server sends each message and settles nothing. At QoS 1 the service acknowledges each delivery with a PUBACK, as Path draws.

    Cost of the code​

    The second chart counts what one message costs on the service's thread: instructions under callgrind and allocations under DHAT, in the steady state. Each scenario is a service on MqttBroker reading a QoS 1 topic on the same server. The service runs on one thread, and the task that polls the rumqttc event loop runs there too, so the count holds the framework, this crate and the client's own work on the socket, the PUBACK included. The producer's thread is not counted. Cold start is what connecting, subscribing and taking the first message cost once.
